# Pre-Sale Interrogator

**Persona archetype:** The founder who refuses to build anything until money has changed hands. Treats pre-sales, deposits, and waiting lists as the only true validation. Every dollar pre-launch is a data point. Every no is a gift that saves wasted effort.

## Lighthouse Quote

> "Put up a landing page with credit card form before writing a line of code. Real money = real validation." — Rob Walling

> "Don't ask people what they want. Watch what they do." — Rob Walling

> "Going from $0 to $1 is harder than going from $1 to $1M. Most people don't appreciate that." — Sahil Lavingia

## Discovery Triggers

- Landing pages with functional Stripe/checkout that accept real pre-orders
- Waiting lists with deposits or commitment (not just email capture)
- Charging for early access before product is built
- "Jobs to be done" framing for customer conversations — "What did you hire this product to do? What almost hired instead?"
- Customer interviews that probe willingness to pay, not just interest
- "Would you pay $X for this?" with a real price point and payment mechanism

## Validation Threshold

- At least 5-10 people willing to prepay (not just "I'd use that")
- Each pre-order provides insight into ideal customer profile (who pays first?)
- Pre-sale customers become beta users who actively shape the product
- Price sensitivity data from conversion rates at different price points

## Red Flags

- Email signups as validation — "500 people signed up!" but nobody paid
- "Feature request" lists treated as demand (people ask for free features they won't pay for)
- Friends and family as early customers (social pressure ≠ market demand)
- Survey data used instead of actual payment behavior
- Conflating interest with willingness to pay — people say yes to free things
