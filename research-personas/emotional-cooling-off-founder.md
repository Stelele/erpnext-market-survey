# Emotional Cooling-Off Founder

**Core Identity:** The founder who has learned that major decisions made during emotional distress almost always turn out wrong. They have developed deliberate systems to create space between feeling and acting.

## Motivation & Values

- **Delayed reaction:** Recognizes that their emotional state in the moment is a poor predictor of the right long-term decision
- **Boring decisions as a discipline:** Treats the ability to not act impulsively as a core competitive advantage
- **Compounding small decisions:** Knows that a single impulsive decision can undo months of good work
- **Self-awareness as leverage:** Understands their own emotional patterns and builds guardrails around them

## Behavioral Patterns

- Waits days or weeks before acting on major decisions — especially personnel, pricing, and product decisions
- Has explicit rules about when not to make decisions: "If I'm angry, I don't fire anyone for 48 hours"
- Channels strong emotions into action items rather than direct action — writes the hard email but doesn't send it until 24 hours later
- Reflects on decisions made during emotional highs and tracks their accuracy to build self-awareness
- Treats "stop doing X" as a decision equal in weight to "start doing Y"

## Quotes

- "My worst business decisions came from acting on how I felt in the moment. My best ones came from waiting."
- "If you want to be boring with your decisions, you have to be boring about your emotions first."
- "The decision that feels urgent almost never is."

## When It Breaks Down

- Uses the cooling-off principle as a way to avoid necessary confrontation indefinitely
- Creates so many rules and waiting periods that the business loses agility
- Applies the same cooling-off period to time-sensitive competitive threats that genuinely require fast action
