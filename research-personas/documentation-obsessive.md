# Documentation Obsessive

**Core Identity:** The founder who treats internal documentation as a primary business asset — not an afterthought, but a competitive moat that compounds in value over time.

## Motivation & Values

- **Business as a system:** Sees the company as a collection of processes that can be documented, repeated, and improved
- **Sellability as a filter:** Documents obsessively because it makes the business more sellable — buyers pay premium prices for businesses they can due-diligence easily
- **Reducing founder dependency:** Every documented process reduces the gap between the founder and the business
- **Test coverage as documentation:** Tests serve double duty — they document expected behavior and prevent regression

## Behavioral Patterns

- Writes documentation before, during, and after every significant process change
- Treats documentation as part of the definition of done — a feature isn't shipped until it's documented
- Maintains separation between business accounts and personal accounts from day one
- Runs cohort analysis to understand customer behavior and document findings
- Creates onboarding documentation so thorough that any competent person could eventually replace them
- Uses financial dashboards that tell the story of the business clearly

## Quotes

- "Documentation is the difference between a business that runs on you and a business that runs itself."
- "If I had to sell tomorrow, what would a buyer need to see? I document that."
- "Tests are documentation that never gets stale."

## When It Breaks Down

- Documents things that don't need documenting, creating busywork and outdated artifacts
- Perfectionism in documentation becomes a procrastination mechanism — shipping is more important than perfect docs
- The documentation doesn't match reality because processes change faster than the docs are updated

## Related Personas

- Small Army Builder — both prepare the business for a team, but the Documentation Obsessive focuses on written knowledge while the Small Army Builder focuses on human capability
- System Buyer — often uses documented systems like EOS as the scaffolding for their own documentation practice
