# Customer Discovery Specialist

## Who They Are

The founder who treats every customer conversation as a structured research opportunity. They are deeply skilled in discovery questioning — not just "what problem do you have" but "what have you tried before," "what almost hired instead," and "what would have to be true for you to pay for this." They understand that most customer feedback is noise and have developed frameworks for extracting signal from conversation, surveys, and community observation.

## What Drives Them

Driven by the insight that the most important marketing decision is what problem you solve for whom — and that you can only know that by talking to customers rigorously. They reject the "build it and they will come" model. They believe that every customer conversation is a data point, and that patterns across conversations reveal opportunities that no amount of market research or competitive analysis can surface.

## Discovery Triggers

- They spend time in communities where their target customer already gathers — forums, Slack workspaces, Reddit threads
- They notice the same complaint appearing in different words from different people
- They find that customers describe problems in terms of solutions ("I need an app that does X") rather than underlying needs
- They hear emotional language — "this drives me crazy," "I waste hours on this" — that signals real pain versus nice-to-have
- They discover that customers have already tried multiple workarounds — they're not solving the problem, just tolerating it

## Validation Threshold

They validate through structure: they use jobs-to-be-done framing ("what did you hire this product to do, what almost hired instead"), they track how often the same problem appears across conversations, and they look for trigger events ("what changed recently that made this a priority"). They discount feature requests and pay attention to workflows and workarounds.

## Red Flags

- Survey data used instead of actual payment behavior — people say they'll pay for things they won't
- Treating one conversation as validation — patterns across multiple conversations are required
- Confusing interest with urgency — "that would be nice" is not the same as "I need this now"
- Asking leading questions that confirm rather than discover — "wouldn't you love a tool that does X?"
- Ignoring the customers who said no — understanding rejection is as important as understanding interest
- Collecting feedback without acting on it — customers notice when their input is ignored

## ERPNext Ecosystem Lens

ERPNext community forums, Reddit r/ERPNext, and ERPNext community calls are rich sources of customer discovery material. The Customer Discovery Specialist in this ecosystem knows how to read these sources for signal — distinguishing between "this one person wants this feature" and "this is a recurring pain point that the community has normalized." They know that ERPNext implementation partners have deep discovery skills from client work that could inform product direction.

**ERPNext opportunity signals:**
- Frappe Forum posts with the same complaint in multiple threads — signals a real, recognized pain
- ERPNext community call topics that generate the most engagement — audience voting reveals pain
- ERPNext Reddit posts with high engagement — the community upvote is a rough validation signal
- ERPNext implementation partners sharing "top 10 client requests" — aggregated discovery
- ERPNext companies describing workarounds — "we use a spreadsheet to do X because ERPNext can't"

**Pain points this persona feels:**
- ERPNext community forums have too much signal-to-noise — hard to distinguish one-offs from patterns
- ERPNext surveys often capture "wish list" rather than "willing to pay" — misread priorities
- ERPNext companies don't share their pain publicly — discovery requires direct outreach
- ERPNext implementation partners have discovery skills but no systematic way to share insights
- ERPNext community feedback cycles are slow — by the time a pain is validated, the market may have moved
