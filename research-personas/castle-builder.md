# Castle Builder

**Persona archetype:** The founder who obsesses over what they own versus what they rent. Deliberately avoids owning infrastructure, capital equipment, and assets that don't compound — but fiercely owns the means of communication, trust relationships, and customer data. The castle is the audience and the relationship.

## Lighthouse Quote

> "Rent everything that depreciates. Own everything that compounds." — (The Bootstrapped Founder #350)

> "The most valuable thing you own is the relationship with your customer — not the server they log into." — (The Bootstrapped Founder #350)

## Discovery Triggers

- Founders who explicitly discuss what to own vs. rent in business architecture
- Building on rented infrastructure (Fly.io, AWS, Frappe Cloud) as a deliberate choice
- Owning the customer relationship through email lists, communities, direct communication channels
- Treating brand and reputation as the primary owned asset
- Avoiding capital expenditure that doesn't provide compounding returns
- Distinguishing between assets that appreciate (audience, data, trust) and those that depreciate (servers, offices, hardware)

## Validation Threshold

- Customer communication channels are owned (email list, community) and not dependent on third-party platforms
- Data portability: customers can export their data, but the relationship remains
- Brand equity measurable through repeat purchase rates and referrals
- Infrastructure costs scale with revenue (no fixed capital overhead)

## Red Flags

- Owning depreciating assets that require maintenance, upgrades, and replacement cycles
- Building on platforms that can change terms, raise prices, or shut down
- Conflating user growth metrics with owned relationships
- Ignoring the compounding value of customer data and historical usage patterns

## ERPNext Ecosystem Lens

Castle Builders in the ERPNext ecosystem would:

- Deploy ERPNext on rented/managed infrastructure (Frappe Cloud, AWS) and focus energy on what matters
- Own the ERPNext community around their apps — email list, Discord, regular newsletter
- Build ERPNext apps that collect valuable usage data (proprietary benchmarks, industry comparisons)
- Position themselves as the trusted advisor for ERPNext implementations (relationship is the moat)
- Avoid investing in ERPNext infrastructure that depreciates (own the expertise, rent the servers)
- Create ERPNext apps that become the "source of truth" for customer data — switching costs through data lock-in

**High-value ERPNext castle-building opportunities:**
- ERPNext app with proprietary benchmarking data (companies using the app contribute to anonymized industry benchmarks)
- ERPNext partner community building (community = the moat, not the product)
- ERPNext data aggregation tools (aggregate across ERPNext instances for industry insights)
- ERPNext reporting apps where the reporting framework becomes industry standard
