# Pricing Psychologist

## Who They Are

The founder who treats pricing as the most powerful product lever in their business — more powerful than features, more powerful than marketing, and more powerful than sales. They understand that pricing is a conversation with the market about value, and that how you price signals what you're building. Anchoring, tier design, value-based pricing, and the psychology of what customers perceive as fair are all deliberate instruments in their toolkit.

## What Drives Them

Driven by the insight that most founders leave enormous amounts of money on the table by pricing too low out of fear, false modesty, or simple inexperience. They believe that the right price is the one the market will bear — not the one that feels comfortable. They are relentless experimenters with pricing, watching conversion rates and churn with the same attention they give to feature development.

## Discovery Triggers

- They notice their trial-to-paid conversion rates and obsess over why people don't convert
- They're uncomfortable with the gap between their value delivered and their price charged
- They've heard "you're too expensive" and realized that sometimes this means "I don't understand the value yet"
- They're benchmarking against competitors' pricing and wondering if they're leaving money on the table
- They've shipped a product, gotten traction, and now realize pricing was an afterthought

## Validation Threshold

They validate pricing through real money — not surveys, not "would you pay $X," but actual conversion rates at different price points. They run pricing experiments deliberately, changing one variable at a time and measuring impact on revenue per user, churn, and conversion. A 30% price increase that drops conversion by 10% is a win.

## Red Flags

- Pricing based on cost-plus rather than value delivered
- Fear-based pricing: choosing low prices to avoid losing customers
- Conflating low price with competitive advantage — cheap is not a moat
- Ignoring churn as a pricing signal — high churn at any price point means something is wrong
- Over-complicating pricing with too many tiers, confusing add-ons, or hidden fees
- Anchoring against their own costs rather than against the customer's alternative

## ERPNext Ecosystem Lens

ERPNext apps have a particular pricing challenge: ERPNext itself is free (self-hosted) and affordable (Cloud), so ERPNext ecosystem apps must justify their price against a platform users already consider inexpensive. The Pricing Psychologist in this ecosystem understands that ERPNext users aren't just buying software — they're buying ERPNext expertise, time savings, and reduced implementation risk.

**ERPNext opportunity signals:**
- ERPNext apps priced at $9-29/month with low conversion — signals misaligned pricing
- ERPNext apps with bait-and-switch pricing (low entry, expensive enterprise tiers)
- Community complaints about ERPNext hosting costs outpacing the value delivered
- Implementation partners pricing custom work hourly rather than value-based
- ERPNext Cloud pricing tiers creating confusion about what's included vs. add-on

**Pain points this persona feels:**
- ERPNext apps struggle to price above $100/month because of ERPNext's own affordability
- The ERPNext community has a bias toward free and open-source, making paid apps a hard sell
- ERPNext users frequently undervalue their own time, making time-savings arguments ineffective
- ERPNext's per-seat pricing creates friction — apps that bill per-seat face adoption resistance
- ERPNext verticals (manufacturing, healthcare) have different willingness-to-pay that generic apps ignore
