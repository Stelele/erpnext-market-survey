# Engineering Marketer

**Persona archetype:** The founder (often an engineer) who applies engineering rigor to marketing — treating each channel as a system with inputs, outputs, and measurable conversion rates. Builds reputation through technical content before launching products. Reputation is the distribution.

## Lighthouse Quote

> "Marketing for micro-SaaS is not about 'growth hacking' — it's about systematically engineering each component (messaging, acquisition, retention, referral) with the same rigor engineers apply to code." — Patrick McKenzie

> "I got $700,000 consulting offers by writing blog posts about things I knew. The blog posts were the marketing." — Patrick McKenzie

> "The most important marketing decision you'll make is what problem you solve for whom. Everything else is tactics." — Patrick McKenzie

> "Your customers have a mental model of how the world works. Work within it, not against it." — Patrick McKenzie

## Discovery Triggers

- Blog posts, conference talks, and open-source contributions as marketing investments years before product launch
- Deep empathy for customer mental models — anthropological approach to understanding how customers think
- Treating conversion rates like performance metrics; A/B testing copy like code
- "Jobs to be done" framing — understanding what customers hired the product to do and what almost hired instead
- Word-of-mouth loops: making it trivially easy for happy customers to refer you (pre-written templates, shareable reports)
- Long time horizons: marketing investments that take 1-3 years to pay off

## Validation Threshold

- Content ranks for search terms target customers actually search
- Consulting offers or inbound leads emerge from content before product launch
- Audience trusts the founder's expertise before they sell anything
- Content generates warm introductions to potential customers
- Referral traffic and attribution to specific customers — relationship-building loop

## Red Flags

- Creating content for its own sake rather than content that serves a specific customer need
- Publishing without tracking (no analytics, no conversion measurement, no funnel)
- Treating "building in public" as marketing without delivering genuine value
- Ignoring customer feedback on messaging — staying attached to technical framing that customers don't relate to
- Short-term content strategy — one-off blog posts with no consistent theme or expertise positioning

## ERPNext Ecosystem Lens

Engineering Marketers in the ERPNext ecosystem would:

- Write deep technical blog posts about ERPNext customizations, Frappe Framework tips, performance optimization
- Create open-source ERPNext apps on GitHub that demonstrate expertise and attract users
- Give talks at Frappe Community Meetups, ERPNext conferences, or virtual summits
- Publish YouTube tutorials on ERPNext workflows that rank for "how to [specific ERPNext task]"
- Contribute to Frappe/ERPNext documentation — knowledge contribution as marketing
- Build ERPNext-integrated tools and write about the process — "How I built X for ERPNext"

**High-value ERPNext engineering marketing topics:**
- "ERPNext Performance Tuning: A Technical Guide" (SEO gold for operations/IT managers)
- "Frappe Framework for Non-Developers" (expands the ERPNext builder community)
- "10 ERPNext Integrations You Should Automate Today" (linkable reference content)
- ERPNext API documentation improvements and tutorials
- ERPNext vs. Odoo vs. SAP comparison guides (engineering-level analysis)
