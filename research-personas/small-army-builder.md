# Small Army Builder

## Who They Are

The founder who builds distribution through a network of agencies, resellers, affiliates, and partners rather than exclusively through direct sales and marketing. They understand that a bootstrapped company cannot hire a sales force, but it can build a small army of motivated distributors who earn referral fees or margins. They treat partnership as product — designing programs, materials, and incentives that make it easy for partners to sell on their behalf.

## What Drives Them

Driven by the leverage problem: one founder can only sell so much directly. They want their revenue to scale without scaling headcount at the same rate. They are strategic about who they recruit as partners — targeting consultants, agencies, and complementary tool vendors who already serve their target customer and have built relationships of trust.

## Discovery Triggers

- They realize their target customer won't buy from a cold outbound email but will buy from their existing IT consultant
- They notice that implementation partners, agencies, and consultants in their space are reselling or white-labeling tools
- They see competitors building partner programs and wonder why bootstrappers don't do the same
- They find that their customer acquisition cost through direct sales is too high to be profitable
- They discover that partners in their space are hungry for new tools to add to their portfolio

## Validation Threshold

They validate partnership demand by approaching 5-10 potential partners before building the program. They offer referral fees or reseller margins and measure whether partners are actually motivated to sell. A partner program only works if partners earn enough to make it worth their time — if the margins are too thin, no one will sell.

## Red Flags

- Building a partner program before the product has proven demand (partners won't sell something that doesn't work)
- Conflating "partners signed up" with "partners are selling" — signups without sales are vanity
- Offering margins so thin that partners have no incentive to prioritize your product
- Neglecting partner support — partners need materials, training, and communication to sell effectively
- Targeting partners who are also competitors (reselling multiple similar tools without focus)
- Building a partner program that requires too much hand-holding — it should work without constant founder involvement

## ERPNext Ecosystem Lens

ERPNext has a massive ecosystem of implementation partners, hosting providers, and development agencies — many of whom are already selling custom ERPNext work and would love to add pre-built apps to their portfolio. The Small Army Builder in this ecosystem sees ERPNext partners as a distribution channel for vertical apps, compliance tools, and integration products.

**ERPNext opportunity signals:**
- ERPNext partners advertising custom development services — potential for productized version
- Frappe Cloud and ERPNext Cloud partners looking for upsell opportunities beyond hosting
- ERPNext implementation agencies with long client relationships but limited recurring revenue
- ERPNext community members asking "can I white-label an ERPNext app"
- Companies building on ERPNext who need distribution and are looking for partners

**Pain points this persona feels:**
- ERPNext implementation partners have one-time revenue from projects, no recurring
- ERPNext partners struggle to monetize their ERPNext expertise beyond billable hours
- Frappe Cloud doesn't have a clear marketplace for distributing partner-built apps
- ERPNext ecosystem lacks a standard reseller or affiliate program infrastructure
- Partner education takes significant time — Frappe/ERPNext has a steep learning curve
