# Data Flywheel Founder

## Who They Are

The founder who treats data collection as a compounding investment — the business decisions they make in year one determine what data they have in year five that they couldn't have had in year one. They understand that proprietary, longitudinal, hard-to-aggregate data is one of the few durable competitive moats available to bootstrapped companies. They build data collection into the product experience itself, not as a separate analytics feature, but as a natural byproduct of usage.

## What Drives Them

Driven by the insight that software can be copied, distribution can be outspent, but proprietary data takes time and compounding to build. They ask: "What data will I have in five years that I don't have today?" and then work backwards to figure out how to start collecting it now. They see their users as giving them gold every time they use the product — most founders don't mine it.

## Discovery Triggers

- They notice their product naturally generates data that becomes more valuable as more users join
- They find that benchmark comparisons (how does this customer's data compare to similar businesses) create stickiness
- They discover that cohort analysis reveals patterns about what behaviors predict retention
- They see that customers value seeing their own historical data in ways they didn't anticipate
- They realize that aggregated industry data (anonymized) could be a product in itself

## Validation Threshold

They validate data value by asking: does this data become more valuable with more users? Can competitors easily replicate this data? Would customers pay specifically for access to this data or the insights derived from it? If the answer to all three is yes, the data flywheel is real.

## Red Flags

- Collecting data that users don't benefit from — surveillance rather than service
- Waiting to collect data until they have a clear plan for using it (start collecting even without a plan)
- Conflating "lots of data" with "valuable data" — not all data compounds
- Ignoring the ethical and legal dimensions of data collection (GDPR, CCPA, industry regulations)
- Building a data moat before product-market fit — data doesn't matter if the product doesn't work
- Treating data as an asset without building the product decisions that actually use it

## ERPNext Ecosystem Lens

ERPNext generates enormous amounts of business data — financial transactions, inventory movements, HR data, manufacturing logs — but ERPNext's native analytics are limited. The Data Flywheel Founder in this ecosystem sees ERPNext data as the raw material for a compounding moat: benchmarks, predictive models, industry comparisons, and automated insights that improve as more ERPNext companies use the product.

**ERPNext opportunity signals:**
- ERPNext companies using third-party BI tools (Metabase, Power BI) to compensate for ERPNext's limited analytics
- Forum posts requesting "ERPNext benchmarking" or "how do I compare my ERPNext data to industry averages"
- ERPNext companies exporting data to spreadsheets for analysis ERPNext can't provide
- Frappe/ERPNext apps that aggregate anonymized data across installations (rare but powerful)
- ERPNext users asking for predictive features (inventory forecasting, cash flow prediction) that require data

**Pain points this persona feels:**
- ERPNext's reporting is backward-looking — no predictive or prescriptive analytics built in
- ERPNext companies have no industry benchmarks — they don't know how their margins compare to similar businesses
- ERPNext data is siloed — no easy way to combine ERPNext data with data from other tools
- ERPNext companies don't realize the value of their own historical data until it's too late
- Frappe's multi-tenancy makes cross-tenant data aggregation difficult (privacy + technical barriers)
