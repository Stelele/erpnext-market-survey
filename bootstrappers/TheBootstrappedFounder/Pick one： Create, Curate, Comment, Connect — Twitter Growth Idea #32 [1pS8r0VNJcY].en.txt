Are you a creator, a curator, a
connector, a consumer, or a commenter?
Which one of these people associate
with you really makes a difference
in what they expect you to do.
Hi, I'm Arvid, and every day I
share one core lesson from my
Twitter course, Find your Following.
Today, I want to talk about the four main
types of Twitter expert that you can be,
or rather that people can think you are.
The role we play really matters
to how people perceive us and what
expectations they build for the
content that we create on Twitter.
If you are a creator, then people
expect you to write or to illustrate or
to create videos, to make new things.
If you are a curator, they don't
necessarily think you need to do new
things, but you should contextualize the
things that other people already create:
in lists or in threads, there are many
ways to curate, to selectively share good
things that other people have created.
If you are a connector, then it's
not so much about the content you
create, but about the community
that you allow to flourish.
So if you like dealing with people, to
connect people, connect with people and to
connect people with each other, then maybe
being a connector is more interesting then
to consistently create or curate things.
And finally, sometimes people just
need somebody to recontextualize
things that other people have created.
And that's the job of the commenter:
somebody that puts existing
content into a new perspective.
All of these four groups can co-exist,
but it is best if you pick one of them
for your Twitter journey, because if you
are precise in what you do, then people
will follow you more easily because
they know what you will present to them.
