As a software entrepreneur, you probably already know about product market fit.
But have you ever considered the medium in which your solution is implemented, and how
that fits into the workflow of your customers?
That's where Solution Workflow Fit comes in.
It's essential to provide a solution in a medium that makes sense for your user.
For instance, consider the difference between a physical book and an ebook.
Both kind of serve the same purpose, but the medium in which they are presented matters
a lot to the consumer.
A physical book may be more comfortable to read, and the tactile experience of holding
the pages can add to the enjoyment of the reading experience.
Then on the other hand, the ebook may just be more convenient in terms of portability
and accessibility, as it can be stored on a single device and doesn't take up any
physical space, and you can play with the font size and all these little things.
You can carry a whole library of reference books with you, if they're digital.
So try that with paper books.
The medium matters.
To achieve a Solution Workflow Fit, you need to understand your target industry's existing
workflows and the tools that your customers use.
That's the medium that they expect solutions to appear in.
For example, farmers.
They need a mobile app to track inventory as they work in the field or in their barns.
They cannot use a web app designed for desktop use.
If you want them to use your software, you need to be accessible where they work.
Similarly audio engineers, they need a solution that seamlessly integrates with their digital
audio workstations.
A web-based SaaS might not be enough if it disrupts their workflow.
Nobody wants to leave the tool that they're comfortable with to use your service.
If you're serving an industry where people use a lot of plugins, you will need to provide
a plugin too.
I made a video on stair-stepping into entrepreneurship by building plugins a few weeks ago, so check
it out.
Now, back to the Solution Workflow Fit.
How do we even know what that workflow is?
To get started, research your target industry's existing workflows.
YouTube is a great source of information here.
Watch tutorials and explainer videos from subject matter experts and then investigate
the tools that they use.
Look at their screens, look at the little buttons and see what they use.
Learn about the file formats and the data transformation expectations in the industry
as well.
Listen to people and see what they mention over and over again.
These common practices will exist for every single one of your prospective users, so you
will need to fit in there.
Then design your solution to actually fit between the existing tools that people use.
Think about the medium in which your solution will be offered, and then ensure that it's
accessible across various platforms and devices.
Your solution should integrate seamlessly into your customer's existing toolchain, and
you need to know which tools to use for that.
Remember, a good product will change an industry's processes and best practices over time, not
overnight.
Disruption to people's existing processes will be a major problem for them.
Even though your solution might be much better than what they have right now, they will be
scared of having to learn everything again from scratch.
Be willing to adapt your solution to fit your customer's needs today and leave your ego
at the door.
Also, consider that some problems may not need a software-based solution at all.
Your business success might lie in creating an info product, like a technical handbook
or process manual, maybe even a glorified checklist, which could lay the groundwork
for future SaaS businesses.
So always think from your customer's perspective to build a product that integrates well into
their professional lives.
Achieving solution workflow fit is essential for creating a successful SaaS business that
truly helps and empowers your customers.
