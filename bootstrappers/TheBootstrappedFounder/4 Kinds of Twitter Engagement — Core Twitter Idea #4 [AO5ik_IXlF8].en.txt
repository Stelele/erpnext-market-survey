Hi, I'm Arvid, and everyday I
share one core Twitter idea from my
Twitter course Find your Following.
Today, I want to talk about the four
kinds of engagement that you can use
as a framework to engage meaningfully
with other people's content.
So when you engage on Twitter,
when you interact with anybody,
anybody else's conversations, you
have four things you can do, and
they are happening on two axes.
One of them is the content axis.
You can zoom into content and
you can zoom out of content.
The other one is the people axis.
You can share and syndicate to a lot of
people or you can invite specific people.
Let me talk about all four real quick.
Zooming into content means asking very
specific questions to give a more detailed
perspective on some piece of content.
Zooming out of content means asking
open-ended questions that put that
particular piece of content  into
context with other things in the field.
If somebody talks about software
engineering, a programming language,
you can zoom in by asking what specific
problems that can solve well, and you
can zoom out by asking how it compares
to other programming languages.
The idea is to give an additional
perspective for the people consuming
that particular piece of content
to see something else, something
new, something interesting.
And that might also get them to follow
you as a source of getting them informed.
Now, if you talk about people, you can
share to a lot of people and expose to
conversation to your own audience and
other people's audiences or you can
invite specific experts that you know,
will contribute to that conversation.
So on the people level, you can either go
big or go precise just as much as you can
zoom in and zoom out on the content scale.
All of these four methods will make you
an interesting person to follow because
you give people a new perspective on
the content that they already consumed.
