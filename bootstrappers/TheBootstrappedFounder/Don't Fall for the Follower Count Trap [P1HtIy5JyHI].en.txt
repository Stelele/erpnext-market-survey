"When a measure becomes a target it ceases to be a good measure."
These are the words, the famous words, of economist
Charles Goodhart. He introduced this concept in 1975 and it still holds true
today, especially in the world of social media.
On platforms like Instagram and Twitter people dream of building these giant
audiences of followers but when follower counts become the
target it leads to undesirable outcomes. In this
video we'll talk about the pitfalls of focusing on follower count,
the negative consequences of over optimizing for it
and some prevention methods to help you build a quality audience on social media.
Let's start with the pitfalls of focusing on follower count.
When you over optimize for follower count you'll end up with a large number
of low quality followers. These followers aren't really interested
in the content you create but are merely following you in the
hopes of receiving a follow back or winning that giveaway that they
followed you for. They're not there for you. They are there
only for themselves. They want something better. You want a
connection. Optimizing for follower count alone does
not create meaningful relationships. When followers are simply a means to an
end you're not acting in their interest just
your own and that will make it impossible for
trust and the real connection to appear. The quality of each relationship matters
not the quantity. It's just like with real life friendships.
When you're in trouble your true and most loyal friends they will be there
for you not the thousands of acquaintances that
you made along the way. We humans we have this need for
meaningful connection and we will put in the work to make
these relationships valuable if we see them being reciprocated.
If you treat new followers only as a number this won't happen.
There's another problem here when you only focus on the size of your audience.
The emotional impact of a dip in your followers
is painful and it becomes very noticeable
when you track them every day on some dashboard.
If you focus on relationships instead this is by far
not as much of a problem. You know it from your own friends and family too.
The natural ebb and flow of relationships is so much more subtle.
Some days people are in a good mood and on other days they are not. We expect
that ups and downs are part of the journey
but if we see that follower count dip even by just one person we freak out.
Loss aversion kicks in and we beat ourselves up
over it. Here's a great example of how focusing
way too much on one metric destroys the very thing it's supposed to represent.
Look at search engine optimization or SEO.
If you try to find a recipe online you will come across someone's life story
leading into what ends up being just a two-step recipe with like three
ingredients. This is what happens when you optimize
only for the google page rank algorithm. The content becomes tailored to the
algorithm not the human that is actually reading it.
So in our efforts to make our recipes more visible on google
we made them less accessible for the people who actually need to cook.
And then we kicked that into a whole other gear because once people thought
they understood the secrets of SEO they started bot farms and traded links
with each other all to please that algorithm instead of
creating more interesting websites. Being ranked on google that says very
little about the quality of your website anymore.
It just tells you how well you play the game of serving the algorithm.
It makes the world a little worse for everyone involved.
So stay off the simple metrics or measure something that doesn't have
discrete real-time numbers like how engaged you feel with your
audience. It's better to use these unmeasurable
factors as the north star for your audience
building. Besides your own enjoyment and how engaged you feel get a
feeling for the quality of your relationships,
the size of the opportunity surface that those relationships create,
and just how much people are willing to reciprocate and show
kindness to another. And that shines a light on
another problem of focusing just on the follower count.
These less measurable but way more specific measurements
are often neglected. It's never just one number that can describe something as
complex as this massive web of interpersonal connections.
So how do we stay clear of this mistake when building an audience?
The key is to be aware of good heart's law and to actively work against it.
It starts with how well we can isolate our follower accounts from our personal
identity. Your worth as a person doesn't rely on
how many followers you have, and a big crowd around you doesn't make
you more important or better at your job. What matters is what you do with and for
the people who care about you. This means creating
content that is valuable, informative, and most importantly
relationship forming. Successful audience builders focus on
engagement rather than follower count. They take the time to respond to
comments and messages, engage in conversations with their
followers honestly and intentionally, and then
create content that encourages engagement and
relationship building at the same time. Remember that building a quality
audience just takes time. It's not something that will happen
overnight and it requires this consistent level of effort and
dedication to happen eventually. When it comes to
engaging with people, remember Immanuel Kant's formula of
humanity from 1785, acts in a way that treats humanity
whether in your own person or the person of any other,
always at the same time as an end, never merely
as a means. Always make your interactions about creating win-win situations for
other people. Building this community of engaged and
interested individuals around you takes patience and persistence, but it's
absolutely worth it. If you focus on engagement rather than follower count,
you will create a successful and sustainable social media presence that
delivers real value to your audience. Thanks for
