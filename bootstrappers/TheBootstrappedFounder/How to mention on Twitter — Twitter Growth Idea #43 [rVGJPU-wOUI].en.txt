Mentioning people on
Twitter is an art form.
Here are my guidelines for
using Twitter handles to
mention people in conversations.
Hey, my name is Arvid and every
day I share one core lesson from my
Twitter course, Find your Following.
Today, I want to talk
about mentioning people.
Now the most important rule on Twitter,
if you want to mention somebody is
use @ and their Twitter handle, handle
obviously, but there is a problem if
you do that as the very first thing in
a tweet, because then Twitter will treat
it like a conversation, like a reply.
So some people put a dot in front
of it or another word, and then
it turns into a regular tweet.
That might be an important thing to
know if you want to mention people.
So with that out of the way,
let's talk about how you can
involve people in conversations.
Usually I do best when I mention
people with a question that allows
them to share their expertise.
So not just mentioning people to
retweet what I'm talking about.
Or not just mentioning people because
I want to get their attention.
I want to give them an opportunity to
contribute to the ongoing conversation.
If you mention other accounts, give
them a chance to do something good.
Don't just mention them for your own
sake, because people who get mentioned
a lot, really don't like this.
If you only put lists of people, if
you just put name under name under
name under name in a tweet, people
might like it, but they probably
won't engage with it much because they
see it as something that you want.
You don't mention them for their sake.
You're mentioning them for yours.
And people who have mentioned
a lot, really don't like that.
So always make it about the conversation,
make it about a contribution and
invite people to conversations where
they can actually help other people.
If you invite people to conversations
where they can help other people that
will show that you are a connector.
You are a multiplexer of knowledge.
And that is a good thing: that
will build a reputation for being a
community centric expert in your field.
