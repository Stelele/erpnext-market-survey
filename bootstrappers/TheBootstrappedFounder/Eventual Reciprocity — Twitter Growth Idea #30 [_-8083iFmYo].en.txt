Delaying gratification is one of the most
effective things you can do on Twitter.
Hi, my name is Arvid and everyday I
share one core idea for my Twitter
course, Find your Following.
Today, I want to talk
about eventual reciprocity.
What is eventual reciprocity?
I have found, particularly on Twitter,
but in most other ways of engaging with
an audience, that if you start by giving
and giving and giving, without asking
for anything in return, eventually
people can't help but give back.
You can find this in
your normal life as well.
If you help people around you in your
family or in your circle of friends
a lot, they will eventually feel like
they need to chip in for that meal,
or they need to show up for your move
and help you carry all those boxes.
People, humans, have this
capacity of taking only so
much help without giving back.
And that happens eventually.
Now on Twitter, that means if you
built relationships with people, by
giving them something, by sharing
your opinions, by helping them reach
their own goals, eventually they will
consider you as somebody that they
might want to give something back to.
And that takes time.
You can see the opposite of this in many
accounts that don't make it on Twitter.
They ask for stuff from day one,
they have a lot of asks and a very
small amount of gives and they don't
get much in return because people
don't want to give back just yet.
They haven't received enough to
feel obligated to return something.
So start with giving a lot, share a lot.
Don't ask for anything just yet.
And over time, people will naturally
feel the need to give back to you.
