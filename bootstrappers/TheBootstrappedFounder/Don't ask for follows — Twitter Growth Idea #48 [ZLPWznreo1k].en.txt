Hey, my name is Arvid and every
day I share one core lesson from my
Twitter course, Find your Following.
Today, I want to talk
about asking for follows.
There is a problem and it's
called follow for follow.
Some people use Twitter as a kind
of transactional exchange medium.
They ask people to please follow them
and promise to follow them in return.
That makes the follow not a relationship,
but just a transaction and no audience
has ever been built on a transaction
and endured the test of time.
If you want to have an engaged audience,
you need people to care about you
and about the things that you have to
say, you will not find those people by
promising to increase their follower
count; you will find those people by
promising and delivering something
meaningful into their Twitter feeds.
So that is how you find
people who want to follow you.
If you have to lure them in, with
the offer of a follow, what kind
of people do you expect to get?
There might be some among them
that actually like what you have
to say, but that is minority.
Most of them are just looking to increase
the vanity metric of Twitter followers.
So don't ask for follows because
people will expect you to follow
back and it will turn the whole
thing into a transactional exchange.
And real relationships
are not just transactions.
They are long-term connections.
Maybe they have transactions
as part of them.
Maybe people buy your product
or you sell them your service.
But that is just one part
of the whole relationship.
So don't ask for follows, make
yourself interesting enough for
people to actually follow you.
